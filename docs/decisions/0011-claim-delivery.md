# ADR-0011: One-time claim delivery

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-10-21

## Context

ADR-0010 left the signed envelope deliberately unpersisted: issuance was
durable, delivery was nothing. A customer who paid still had no way to
receive the credential, and "signed but never delivered" was invisible.
The remaining M1 question was how signed bytes become a receivable claim
without creating a second lineage revision or a second authorization path.

## Decision

SQLite schema v4 adds two tables next to the lineage evidence:

- `commerce_issued_credential` is append-only and stores the exact signed
  envelope bytes (key id, payload, signature as Permit serializes them),
  bound uniquely to the issuance outbox row and to the lineage revision.
  Triggers reject updates and deletes.
- `commerce_claim` is the one-time fulfillment state: the SHA-256 digest of
  a random claim token, the credential it unlocks, status
  (`ready`/`claimed`/`expired`), notification time, claim time, and the
  fulfillment window. Deletion is rejected; only status transitions occur.

The claim token is a 128-bit random value generated by the worker, returned
exactly once to the caller for a single customer handoff, and stored only
as its digest. It is a distinct trust domain from provider secrets and from
Permit signing keys: possessing a valid, unexpired token fulfills one
credential, nothing else.

Completion gains the delivery half atomically: when the worker supplies the
signed envelope bytes, key id, claim token, and expiry, the same short
transaction that appends the revision and completes the outbox row also
records the issued credential and its claim. A completed issuance therefore
always has its delivery evidence, or the caller explicitly recorded none.

Fulfillment is `storage-claim-credential`: one transaction verifies the
token digest, status, and window, marks the claim `claimed`, and returns
the exact signed envelope bytes. It never creates or advances a lineage
revision. `storage-mark-claim-notified` records that a transport handed the
token out; the first timestamp wins so retries stay idempotent. No HTTP
listener is part of this decision — transports and the claim portal are
separate bounded slices.

## Failure and retry contract

- Crash after signing but before completion: unchanged from ADR-0010; the
  reclaiming worker re-signs identical bytes. Now the completion also
  carries the envelope, so no signed work is ever lost.
- Wrong, reused, or expired token: typed failures (`claim-not-found`,
  `claim-already-claimed`, `claim-expired`) that mutate nothing. Expired
  claims keep their row for audit; routine expiry handling is operator and
  retention work.
- Delivery transport failure after the token was generated: the claim stays
  `ready` and the caller retries notification; fulfillment is unaffected.
  Notification is recorded through the idempotent mark, never by re-issuing.

## Consequences

- The M1 loop is closed end to end for one-time purchases: paid webhook →
  verified event → projected order → signed revision → claimable
  credential, each step durably evidenced.
- The customer-facing surface is still only a storage primitive; an HTTP
  edge, notifications, and a portal remain future slices.
- Retention of expired claims and issued credentials needs a versioned,
  audited operation and stays future work.
